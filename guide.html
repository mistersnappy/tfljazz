ISS Telemetry & Sound Mapping Guide

This document breaks down how live data from the International Space Station directly drives the procedural audio engine and soundscape in real time.

1. Distance to You (Proximity Reverb)

What it is: The direct great-circle distance (calculated via the Haversine formula) between your IP location and the live coordinates of the ISS.

Musical Effect: Controls the Reverb Wet/Dry Mix. When the station orbits far away from you across the globe, the sound blooms into a massive, heavily reverberant space wash. When it orbits closer to your region, the sound tightens up to feel dry, intimate, and direct.

2. Visibility: Daylight vs. Eclipses (Emotional Shift)

What it is: Indicates whether the ISS is currently flying through direct sunlight or sitting in Earth's shadow.

Musical Effect: Drives the Major/Minor Scale Morphing. When the station enters daylight, the drone palette shifts into a bright, uplifting Major chord set. When it plunges into Earth's night eclipse, the music transitions into introspective Minor chord structures.

3. Altitude (Timbral Resonance)

What it is: The precise height of the station above Earth (fluctuating between 400 and 420 km due to atmospheric drag).

Musical Effect: Controls the Filter Resonance ($Q$). As the ISS subtly breathes higher or lower in orbit, the peak resonance of the low-pass filter tightens or softens, shifting the overtone brightness and timbral bite of the generative pads.

4. Footprint (Spatial Panning)

What it is: The diameter of the visible horizon area visible from the station (typically around 4,400 to 4,600 km).

Musical Effect: Drives the Stereo Panning Engine and kick transient filter. As the horizon span shifts, the sound gently drifts across your left and right speakers, creating a wide, breathing spatial soundstage.

5. Orbital Velocity (Rhythm & Tempo)

What it is: The speed of the space station as it hurtles along at roughly 27,600 km/h.

Musical Effect: Modulates the Sub-Bass Drum Pulse. Tiny fluctuations in speed subtly adjust the groove tempo and trigger syncopated orbital ghost notes when the station accelerates.

6. Voice Waypoints (Capsule Computer)

What it is: An onboard coordinate scanner tracking major world cities against current orbital coordinates.

Musical Effect: When the ISS flies within 350 km of a major city or landmark, the browser's native text-to-speech engine softly announces the waypoint out loud (e.g., *"Passing over Tokyo"*), acting as an automated space mission audio log.
